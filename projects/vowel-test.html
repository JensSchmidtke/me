German Vowel Test

A pronunciation test for learners of German. The learner reads short words aloud, and an acoustic analysis shows which vowels need practice.

Try it

The test runs on Hugging Face (privacy policy). Your recordings are sent there for analysis and deleted immediately afterwards.

Privacy details

Responsible: Jens Schmidtke (contact). What is sent: your voice recordings of the test words and, as with any web request, your IP address. Recipient: Hugging Face, Inc. (USA), which hosts the analysis program. Purpose: measuring your vowels and showing you the result. Storage: each recording is deleted right after it has been measured; no recordings or results are kept. Your rights: since nothing is kept, there is nothing to access or delete afterwards; for Hugging Face's own server logs, please contact Hugging Face. You can also complain to a data protection authority.

If the test has not been used for a while, starting it can take up to a minute.

How it works

The test covers the 14 stressed vowels of standard German, with two or three words per vowel. Each recording is analysed with Praat (through Parselmouth): the program finds the vowel and measures its duration and its first two formants, which reflect tongue height and tongue position. The learner's values are normalised and compared with reference values for German (Pätzold & Simpson, 1997).

The evaluation checks whether a vowel is close enough to its target, whether rounded vowels (ü, ö) are kept apart from unrounded ones (i, e) and from back vowels (u, o), and whether long vowels are clearly longer than their short counterparts. The written words mark vowel length with a line (long) or a dot (short), as in many German textbooks, so the test measures pronunciation rather than knowledge of spelling rules.

Status

This is a working prototype. The thresholds for flagging a vowel are provisional and are being calibrated with recordings of native speakers.
