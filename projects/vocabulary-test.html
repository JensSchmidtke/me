Adaptive Vocabulary Test

In this project I develop a vocabulary test that estimates a person's vocabulary knowledge and places the result on a level of the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). The reliability of the result, that is, how precisely it pins down a person's level, depends on the number of items a test taker answers: the more items, the more precise the estimate. But a test with enough items for a precise result would be long. An adaptive test offers a compromise. It stays short because each test taker is shown only items close to their own level, so nobody spends time on items that are far too easy or far too hard. The result is shown as a point estimate on a scale from 0 to 100, with error bars showing the uncertainty of the estimate.

The test is still under development, but the interim results show that a short test of about five minutes can estimate a person's level reliably (reliability of .87), that these estimates agree well with an established vocabulary test, the LexTALE (correlation of .78), and that the items cover the whole range of ability, from beginners to very advanced learners.

Next step

The next step is linking the results to the CEFR by establishing cut-offs, for example by asking experienced teachers to judge which words a learner at each level should know, or by comparing the results of learners whose level is known from their courses or from external exams.

Teachers and institutions interested in using the test with their learners are welcome to get in touch.
